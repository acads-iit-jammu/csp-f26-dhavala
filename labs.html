Labs

The Computer Lab (Slot FL, 1 credit, 0-0-2) runs for two hours each week:

Branch Day Time Room
Electrical Tuesday 2:00–4:00 pm Lab 4022
Mechanical Wednesday 2:00–4:00 pm Lab 4022
Metallurgy & Materials Wednesday 2:00–4:00 pm Lab 4021

Bring your laptop if you have one. You can also work in the browser with OnlineGDB — no installation needed. We will show you how to set up a local C toolchain (gcc/clang) in the first lab.

Evaluation: regular lab work counts for 20% of the course grade (see Assessment). Lab 1 is a setup session and is not graded; every lab from Lab 2 onwards is evaluated with equal weight.

Lab Schedule

The schedule below is tentative and covers all 11 practical modules of the syllabus (the module column refers to the syllabus practical table). Lab handouts are self-contained: where a lab reaches a construct before its full lecture treatment, the handout introduces just enough of it to do the exercises. Because institute holidays fall on different weekdays, the Tuesday (EE) and Wednesday (Mech/Materials) batches drift by a week in places — go by the session number, not the date.

# EE (Tue) Mech & Materials (Wed) Topic Module
1 Aug 4 Aug 5 Basic input/output programs (incl. toolchain & editor setup) 1
2 Aug 18 Aug 19 First C programs: printf/scanf, variables, operators — graded set e1–e5 1
3 Aug 25 Sep 2 Solid ground — consolidation: types, operators, precedence, integer vs. real division 1
4 Sep 1 Sep 9 Loops: for, while and do-while, and converting between them 5, 6
5 Sep 8 Sep 16 Lists and strings: arrays, strings as character arrays 3
6 Sep 15 Sep 30 Using libraries (string.h, math.h, etc.) 10
7 Sep 29 Oct 7 Functions and recursion 7
8 Oct 6 Oct 14 Tuples & dictionaries, C-style: structures and key–value lookup tables 4
9 Oct 13 Oct 21 File handling 8
10 Oct 27 Oct 28 Error handling 9
11 Nov 3 Nov 4 Problem solving I (GATE / ACM ICPC style) 11
12 Nov 17 Nov 18 Problem solving II 11

No lab on: Wed Aug 26 (Milad-un-Nabi), Tue Sep 22 & Wed Sep 23 (mid-semester exams), Tue Oct 20 (Dussehra), Tue Nov 10 & Wed Nov 11 (holidays).

The syllabus allocates 28 practical hours (8 of them for problem solving); holidays leave 12 sessions = 24 hours. Two adjustments close the gap: modules 5 and 6 are taught together in Lab 4 (for, while and do-while are one idea in three costumes, and converting between them is a better exercise than meeting them a week apart), and the remaining problem-solving hours are covered through a make-up lab session (date TBA) plus problems embedded in earlier sheets — Lab 3 alone carries 24 practice problems.

  • Search & sort visualizations — see linear vs. binary search in action.
  • MIT OCW 6.087 labs and assignments — extra practice problems with solutions.
