Why does changing n in one call not change another call’s n?

Each invocation has its own parameter and local variables. A caller is suspended while a callee runs. Returning completes the callee and supplies its value to the caller.

Is recursion always faster?

No. The recursive maximum here does linear work with linear call depth. Splitting into halves still needs linear total work but can reduce depth to logarithmic. Naive Fibonacci recomputes the same subproblems; its call count grows rapidly.

Logical call frames are shown, not physical memory addresses. Optimisation may change physical execution. Examples use deliberately small inputs to avoid integer overflow and excessive depth. A function call does not allocate a new copy of an array argument.
