The Toy Computer

an idealized machine — 40 memory cells, one accumulator, and nothing to hide · CS-1-01 (MO), IIT Jammu

What you are looking at. A computer, honestly, is just three things: a row of numbered boxes that hold numbers (the memory — the 40 cells in the middle panel), one scratch box the machine does all its arithmetic in (the ACC, for accumulator), and a finger that always points at the box holding the next order to follow (the PC, program counter). A program is nothing more than orders stored in the boxes, followed one at a time.

Do this now:

  1. Leave the first example ("1 · Sequence — add two numbers") loaded, and look at the left panel: that is the plan, written in the machine's tiny vocabulary.
  2. Press Step once. Watch: the blue-ringed cell is where the finger points; the diary (right panel) tells you in plain words what just happened.
  3. Keep pressing Step and predict each move before you make it. When the answer appears in the output tray, you have watched a program run — all of it, no magic.

Then work through the examples in order, 1 → 6. It is one single story: adding two integers, told with one new idea per episode. Green cell = just read, orange = just written.

1 · Write a plan (program)

Instruction card (the machine's whole vocabulary)
INPtake the next number from the input tray into the accumulator
OUTcopy the accumulator to the output tray
LDA aload: copy cell a into the accumulator
STA astore: copy the accumulator into cell a
ADD aadd cell a to the accumulator
SUB asubtract cell a from the accumulator
BRA abranch: next instruction comes from cell a
BRZ abranch to a only if the accumulator is 0
BRP abranch to a only if the accumulator is ≥ 0
HLThalt
DAT vnot an instruction — reserves a cell holding value v

Encoding: each cell holds a 3-digit number. 1aa=ADD, 2aa=SUB, 3aa=STA, 5aa=LDA, 6aa=BRA, 7aa=BRZ, 8aa=BRP, 901=INP, 902=OUT, 000=HLT. Instructions and data are both just numbers — the machine cannot tell them apart.
