Lab 04: Loops

Electrical Mechanical Metallurgy & Materials
Date Tue Sep 1 Wed Sep 9 Wed Sep 9
Time & Room 2:00–4:00 pm · Lab 4022 2:00–4:00 pm · Lab 4022 2:00–4:00 pm · Lab 4021

Lab pack: lab04.tar.gz — pre-placed on lab machines, downloadable for home practice.

Syllabus practical modules: 5 and 6 — for loops, and while / do-while loops. They are taught together on purpose: the three forms are one idea in three costumes, and converting between them is a better exercise than meeting them a week apart.

Evaluation: Graded — equal weight within the 20% lab component. Ten drills, five fill-in-the-blank programs, and three checked problems, plus observed work and a short viva. Teams of 1–2; pairs swap driver/navigator at half-time.

Lab manual: The C Programming Laboratory — the department’s shared reference (vim §1 · the compilation model §2.5 · types and #define §2.7–2.8 · the operator precedence table §2.9–2.10 · printf/scanf §3). This sheet is self-contained; the manual is there when you want more.

What changes today

You write the scanf now. Until last week the reading was done for you and you supplied the thinking. From today the starters give you the declarations and the final printf — nothing else. The scaffolding comes away one plank at a time; this is the first plank.

And the starters do not compile as shipped. That is deliberate. Every blank is a name the compiler has never heard of, so instead of a wrong answer you get a message naming the exact line still waiting for you:

e3.c:19:5: error: use of undeclared identifier 'TODO_read_a_and_b'

Act 0 · Land (10 min)

Pack pre-placed on machines, or:

wget https://acads-iit-jammu.github.io/csp-f26-dhavala/labs/lab04.tar.gz
tar xzf lab04.tar.gz
cd lab04
./install.sh

It puts you inside your workspace and changes your prompt to prove it. Then start the recorder:

script session.txt

Do not go further until your prompt shows [lab04 OK ...]. If it does not, run ./where.sh — it changes nothing, and it prints the exact line to copy to get back.

Act 1 · Drills — predict, then run (25 min)

Ten drills. For each: see it, write down what you think happens, and only then run it.

./drill.sh --all
Drills Territory
t01 one type conversion, to keep it warm — does a float variable rescue an int division?
p01–p04 precedence, aimed straight at loop conditions: a comparison is a value; 0 < n < 3 lies; < vs <= at the boundary; and = is not ==
L1–L5 the compiler pipeline, by hand — the four stages from Monday’s lecture, one command each, ending at nm and the U printf that explains why linkers exist

A wrong prediction scores exactly as much as a right one — but only if you write it down first. L1–L5 are self-marked; marking yourself right when you were not simply throws away the one record that shows you what to revise.

Act 2 · Fill the blanks (25 min)

Five programs, each missing its scanf and the lines that make the loop move.

File What it does
b1_sum_while.c add 1 + 2 + … + n, with a while
b2_sum_for.c the same sum, as a for — convert it; do not copy it
b3_digits.c how many digits does n have? (/10 until it runs out)
b4_factorial.c n! — and the GIVEN stops at 12. Work out why
b5_digits0.c digits again, but n may be 0

Do b3 first, then feed your answer 0. It says zero digits — wrong, because a while checks before it runs, so the body never runs at all. b5 is the same problem with do-while, which checks after. That contrast is the entire reason C has three loops instead of one.

Act 3 · The checked three (40 min, solo)

Problem Task Final line ends with exactly
e1.c sum and count of the even numbers up to n sum=<S> count=<C>
e2.c reverse the digits of n rev=<R>
e3.c greatest common divisor of two numbers gcd=<G>

e2 has a public example worth reading twice: 900 → rev=9. Reversed, 900 is 009, and a number has no leading zeros. The checker will draw numbers ending in zero.

e3 is Euclid, 300 BC. The whole algorithm rests on one fact — gcd(a, b) is the same number as gcd(b, a % b). Check it by hand on 48 and 18 before you write anything. The pair keeps changing; the answer never does. That unchanging thing is a loop invariant, and it is why you may stop the moment b reaches 0.

How checking works — as before: ./check.sh e1 compiles your source and runs it on the public examples and on fresh random inputs drawn from the GIVEN. A program that memorises an example fails the next draw. New this week: if the checker says TIMEOUT, your loop never ends — nothing is wrong with the machine.

Act 4 · Check, fix, bundle (10 min)

./check.sh

Fix, re-check, repeat. Then — green or time-up (an imperfect bundle beats no bundle):

./check.sh --bundle

Carry ../lab04_<rolls>_<branch>_w<NN>.tar.gz out with you (Moodle + email yourself). The machine forgets.

Stretch (fast finishers)

  • The triangle, properly. Lab 02’s stretch made you print a 4-row triangle with one printf per line, because you had no loops. Write it again with nested loops, and make the number of rows something the user types. Then read the two versions side by side — that diff is three weeks of progress.
  • Convert all three. Rewrite e1 as a while, e2 as a for, e3 as a do-while. One of the three will fight you. Which, and why?
  • Count the rounds. Add a counter to e3 and print how many times the loop ran for 48 18, then for two large numbers close together. Euclid is fast — how fast?

Reflect

✍️ Into notes/answers.txt: look at notes/drills.log and pick the drill you got most wrong. In two sentences — what did you believe before you ran it, and what do you believe now?
