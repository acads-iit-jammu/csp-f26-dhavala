Lab pack: lab05.tar.gz — pre-placed on lab machines, downloadable for home practice.

Syllabus practical module: 2 — Writing programs related to conditional statements

Evaluation: Graded — equal weight within the 20% lab component. Ten drills, five fill-in-the-blank programs, and three checked problems, plus observed work and a short viva. Teams of 1–2; pairs swap driver/navigator at half-time.

Lab manual: The C Programming Laboratory — the department’s shared reference (vim §1 · the compilation model §2.5 · types and #define §2.7–2.8 · the operator precedence table §2.9–2.10 · printf/scanf §3). This sheet is self-contained; the manual is there when you want more.

The one sentence to leave with

break leaves exactly one construct — the nearest enclosing loop or switch.

Almost everything that goes wrong today is that sentence, not yet believed. You will meet it again next week, when a break in the inner loop of a matrix search does not stop the outer one.

Act 0 · Land (10 min)

wget https://acads-iit-jammu.github.io/csp-f26-dhavala/labs/lab05.tar.gz
tar xzf lab05.tar.gz
cd lab05
./install.sh

Then start the recorder:

script session.txt

Do not go further until your prompt shows [lab05 OK ...]. If it does not, run ./where.sh — it changes nothing, and prints the exact line to copy.

Act 1 · Drills — predict, then run (25 min)

./drill.sh --all
Drills Territory
d01–d02 the two classic if bugs: one equals sign, and an else that belongs to the wrong if
d03–d04 switch: fall-through with no break, and what %2 does to a negative number
d05–d07 break and continue, and where they actually land
d08–d10 short-circuit &&, a ladder in the wrong order, and a switch that matches nothing

d07 never stops. That is deliberate, and it is the point. Every drill run is capped at five seconds so it cannot hang the machine — but predict before you run it, and look carefully at where i++ lives.

A wrong prediction scores exactly as much as a right one — but only if you write it down first.

Act 2 · Fill the blanks (25 min)

File What it does
b1_evenodd.c even or odd, with if/else — and it is tested on negatives
b2_vowel.c vowel or consonant — a switch where fall-through is the point: five cases, one body
b3_ticket.c ticket price by age bracket — an else-if ladder
b4_grade.c a grade from marks — the same shape, built in the opposite direction
b5_calc.c a mini calculator — switch on a character, every case with its own break

b2 and d03 use the same mechanism and reach opposite verdicts. In d03 the missing break is a bug; in b2 the shared body is the feature. Be able to say why.

Act 3 · The checked three (40 min, solo)

Problem Task Final line ends with exactly
e1.c classify a triangle from three sides type=equilateral | type=isosceles | type=scalene | type=invalid
e2.c is n prime? if not, its smallest divisor n=<N> prime or n=<N> divisor=<D>
e3.c a PIN, and three attempts access=granted tries=<N> or access=denied

e1 — check validity first. Three lengths make a triangle only if every pair adds up to more than the third: 5 5 8 is a triangle, 2 3 9 is three sticks that will not meet. Then ask of your ladder: do my cases cover every possibility?

e2 — search upward from 2 and break the moment you find a divisor; the spec asks for the smallest, and there is no reason to keep looking. Then look at your upper limit: going to n-1 works, and stopping at i*i <= n also works — for 97 that is 9 passes instead of 95. Add a counter and see.

e3 — a loop that can end for two different reasons: the right PIN (you break out) or the attempts running out (the condition ends it). After the loop, your program has to know which happened. That is exactly what the two output lines are asking you to prove.

How checking works — ./check.sh e1 compiles your source and runs it on the public examples and on fresh random inputs drawn from the GIVEN. A program that memorises an example fails the next draw.

Act 4 · Check, fix, bundle (10 min)

./check.sh

Fix, re-check, repeat. Then — green or time-up:

./check.sh --bundle

Carry ../lab05_<rolls>_<branch>_w<NN>.tar.gz out with you (Moodle + email yourself).

Stretch (fast finishers)

  • The bank menu. A while loop showing a menu — balance, deposit, withdraw, quit — with a switch on the choice. Make quit actually quit. If your first attempt keeps looping, you have just rediscovered d05 with your own hands, which is the best way to learn it.
  • Count the passes in e2. Compare i < n against i*i <= n for n = 97 and n = 99.
  • Break e1 on purpose. Find three sides where your program says scalene but the right answer is invalid. Then explain why the order of your tests caused it.

Reflect

✍️ Into notes/answers.txt: in d05 the break ran and the loop kept going. In one sentence — what exactly did that break leave?
