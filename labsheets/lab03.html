Lab 03: Solid Ground

Electrical Mechanical Metallurgy & Materials
Date Tue Aug 25 Wed Sep 2 Wed Sep 2
Time & Room 2:00–4:00 pm · Lab 4022 2:00–4:00 pm · Lab 4022 2:00–4:00 pm · Lab 4021

Lab pack: lab03.tar.gz — pre-placed on lab machines, downloadable for home practice.

Evaluation: graded — equal share of the 20% lab component. Three graded problems, five fill-in-the-blank programs, and sixteen drills, plus observed work and a short viva. Teams of 1–2; pairs swap driver/navigator at half-time.

Nothing new today. No new constructs, no new syntax. Everything here is Week 3 — types, variables, assignment, operators and precedence — met again, from a different angle, until it is automatic. Lab 02 asked you to write programs. Today mostly asks you to predict them, which is harder and more useful.

Lab manual: The C Programming Laboratory — the department’s shared reference (vim §1 · the compilation model §2.5 · types and #define §2.7–2.8 · the operator precedence table §2.9–2.10 · printf/scanf §3). This sheet is self-contained; the manual is there when you want more.

The two things that went wrong last time

We fixed both in the pack, so read this once:

1. People got lost. So this week the installer does not tell you where to go — it puts you there, and changes your prompt to prove it:

[lab03 OK w04-l03-ee-sd]$

If you can see [lab03 OK ...] at the left of your cursor, you are in the right place. If you cannot, you are not. There is now exactly one command to remember, and it works from anywhere at all:

./where.sh

It never changes anything. It tells you where you are, where your workspace is, and prints the exact line to copy to get back.

2. People lost marks on formatting, not on thinking. So this week the final printf is already written for you in every file. You cannot get the output format wrong, because you are not writing it. If the checker says WRONG, it means your computation is wrong — which is a much more useful thing to be told.

Act 0 · Land (10 min)

Pack pre-placed on machines, or:

wget https://acads-iit-jammu.github.io/csp-f26-dhavala/labs/lab03.tar.gz
tar xzf lab03.tar.gz
cd lab03
./install.sh

It will drop you inside your workspace with the new prompt. Then start the recorder:

script session.txt

The transcript travels in your bundle and is the proof of your own work.

Do not go any further until your prompt shows [lab03 OK ...]. If it does not, run ./where.sh and paste the line it gives you.

Act 1 · Drills — predict, then run (35 min)

Sixteen tiny programs. For each one you will see the code, write down what you think it prints, and only then run it.

./drill.sh --all

Predicting is the exercise. Running is only the marking. A wrong prediction is worth exactly as many marks as a right one — but only if you actually write it down first, so be honest with yourself. The one thing that earns nothing is running the program before you have committed to an answer.

What they cover:

Drills Territory
d01–d04 integer vs. real division, % with negatives, where a cast goes
d05–d08 precedence, associativity, *=, chained assignment
d09–d12 characters as numbers, truncation, deriving the case gap
d13–d14 what printf rounds, pads, and refuses to add
s1–s2 (stretch) a comparison is a value; why % refuses floats

./drill.sh on its own shows your progress. ./drill.sh 7 re-runs just number 7.

Drill 04 is the single most expensive misconception in this course. If you get it wrong, stop and stare at it until it is obvious — it comes back in f2, f5 and e2.

Act 2 · Fill in the blanks (35 min)

Five complete programs. The declarations are written, the scanf is written, the final printf is written. Only the expressions are missing, marked /* TODO */.

File Program Blanks
f1_money.c paise → rupees and paise 2
f2_average.c average of three integers, 2 decimals 1
f3_swap.c swap two variables 3 — and the order matters
f4_upper.c a lowercase letter → uppercase + its code 2
f5_temp.c Celsius → Fahrenheit 1

Check them one at a time as you go:

./check.sh f1

The shipped files do not compile until you fill the blanks — that is expected, and the compiler will point straight at the /* TODO */ you have not done yet.

Act 3 · The graded three (30 min, solo)

Same shape: everything written except the computation.

Problem Task Final line ends with exactly
e1.c total inches → feet and inches feet=<F> inches=<I>
e2.c percentage scored, 2 decimals pct=<P>
e3.c an uppercase letter → lowercase + its code lower=<C> code=<N>

Each one is a relative of something you have already done today: e1 is f1 with a different divisor, e2 is the f2 trap in new clothes, and e3 is f4 held up to a mirror — same gap, opposite direction. Work out the direction rather than guessing it.

How checking works — as in Lab 02: ./check.sh e1 compiles your source and runs it on the public inputs from the spec and on fresh random inputs drawn from the GIVEN. A program that memorises the example fails the next draw. Only one that computes survives.

Act 4 · Check, fix, bundle (10 min)

./check.sh

Fix, re-check, repeat. Then — green or time-up (an imperfect bundle beats no bundle):

./check.sh --bundle

Carry ../lab03_<rolls>_<branch>_w<NN>.tar.gz out with you (Moodle + email yourself). The machine forgets.

Stretch (fast finishers)

  • Do drills s1 and s2 if you skipped them.
  • Round, don’t truncate. e1 throws away the leftover inches. Using only /, % and +, print the number of feet rounded to the nearest foot instead. No if, no library. (Hint: what happens if you add half a foot before dividing?)
  • Break f2. Find three inputs in the GIVEN where an average computed with /3 and one computed with /3.0 differ by as much as possible. Put them in notes/answers.txt.

Reflect

✍️ Into notes/answers.txt: look back at your notes/drills.log. Pick the one drill you got most wrong. In two sentences — what did you believe before you ran it, and what do you believe now?
